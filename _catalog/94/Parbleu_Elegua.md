Parbleu returns with the new single ”Elegua”, backed by ”Peut-être Fous”, offering a first glimpse into the music that the Naples-based five-piece collective, with Andres Balbucea and Andrea De Fazio at the creative helm, has been crafting over the past several years. Together, the two tracks distill the band’s distinctive blend of Mediterranean futurism and equatorial groove into irresistible dancefloor excursions.

”Elegua” is a percussive, trance-driven piece built on layered Afro-Caribbean rhythms, circular harmonic motion and warm foundation of analogue synthesizers. Conceived as a ritual opening, it creates a gateway into a playful and spiritual sonic world shaped by polyrhythms and Yoruba call-and-response vocals inspired by traditional Orisha praise chants. Invoking Elegua, the Orisha of crossroads and destiny in the Santería tradition, the track is elevated by a children’s choir whose ceremonial presence amplifies its ancestral spirit while carrying an unmistakable sense of joy.

”Peut-être Fous”, by contrast, pairs nostalgic harmonies with a steady club groove. A funk-inflected electric bass, doubled by synth bass, provides a warm, weighty foundation for the delicate plucked lines of the ngoni, a traditional West African string instrument. Classical guitar and analogue synthesizers add shimmering harmonic layers, resulting in a tropical disco track where hypnotic grooves, nostalgia and surreal warmth coexist effortlessly.
